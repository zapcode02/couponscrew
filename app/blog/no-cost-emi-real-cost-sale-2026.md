# No Cost EMI in the Sale: What It Actually Costs and How to Check

**No Cost EMI usually costs a little, not nothing.** The interest is real, but the seller covers it with an upfront discount. What you still pay is 18% GST on that interest, any processing fee your bank charges, and sometimes a bank discount you would have got by paying in full. On a ₹60,000 phone over six months, that can add up to around ₹700.

*Last updated: 5 October 2026. Amazon Great Indian Festival starts on 8 October and Flipkart Big Billion Days on 9 October.*

## How No Cost EMI actually works

No Cost EMI is a normal EMI loan where the seller pays the interest for you. Your bank still charges interest on the purchase. The seller, or the brand, gives you a discount equal to that interest, so your monthly instalments add up to the product's price.

Here is what happens at checkout, step by step:

1. You pick a product priced at, say, ₹60,000 and choose 6-month No Cost EMI.
2. The seller gives an instant discount equal to the interest the bank will charge over six months.
3. Your card is charged the discounted amount, and the bank converts it into an EMI loan.
4. The bank adds interest to that loan. Your six instalments come to ₹60,000 in total.
5. The bank also charges 18% GST on the interest, and in many cases a one-time processing fee. These are not covered by the discount.

The Reserve Bank of India made its position on "zero per cent" loans clear in a September 2013 circular to banks, saying that "zero per cent interest is non-existent". That is why banks still calculate interest on No Cost EMI purchases. The "no cost" part is the seller's discount, not the absence of interest.

## Worked example: a ₹60,000 phone on No Cost EMI

The numbers below use two assumptions so you can see the mechanics: an annual interest rate of 15% and a processing fee of ₹199 plus GST. Your bank's actual rate and fee may be different; check them on the payment page before you confirm.

| Tenure | Monthly EMI | Seller discount (equal to interest) | GST on interest | Processing fee with GST | Extra you pay |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 months | ₹20,000 | ₹1,469 | ₹264 | ₹235 | about ₹500 |
| 6 months | ₹10,000 | ₹2,540 | ₹457 | ₹235 | about ₹690 |
| 12 months | ₹5,000 | ₹4,603 | ₹829 | ₹235 | about ₹1,060 |

Two things stand out. First, the extra cost grows with the tenure, because a longer loan means more interest and more GST on it. Second, the extra cost is small compared with the price, roughly 1% to 2% here. No Cost EMI is not a trap; it simply is not free.

If your bank charges no processing fee for that offer, the extra cost drops to the GST alone. Some sale offers waive the fee, so check before you choose.

## The bigger cost: the discount you might lose

In festive sales, the larger cost of EMI is often not the GST. It is the difference between the bank offer for full payment and the bank offer for EMI.

Bank instant discounts in big sales sometimes come with different terms for EMI. The percentage may be the same, but the cap or the minimum order can differ, and some offers apply only to full payment. This year, Amazon has said its 10% SBI card discount covers EMI transactions as well as full payments. Flipkart has listed Axis Bank and ICICI Bank card offers. In both cases, the exact caps and EMI terms appear on the product page during the sale.

Before you choose EMI, compare two numbers on the payment page:

- **Final price if you pay in full**, after the bank discount.
- **Total of all EMIs plus processing fee and GST**, after the bank discount for EMI.

If the full-payment option is cheaper by more than a few hundred rupees and you can afford it, EMI is costing you money. If the two are close, EMI is mainly a way to spread the payment, which can be worth it.

## Other costs to check before choosing EMI

### Your credit limit gets blocked

When you convert a purchase into EMI, the full amount is blocked on your card. It is released bit by bit as you pay each instalment. On a card with a ₹1 lakh limit, a ₹60,000 EMI purchase leaves you about ₹40,000 for everything else that month. If you plan to buy more in the same sale, check your available limit first.

### Credit utilisation and your credit score

A large blocked amount raises your credit utilisation, the share of your limit you are using. High utilisation can lower your credit score for a while. Paying every instalment on time helps your score; a missed instalment hurts it and may cancel the No Cost benefit.

### Closing the EMI early

If you pay off the EMI before the tenure ends, many card issuers charge a foreclosure fee, often a small percentage of the outstanding amount. You may also lose the No Cost benefit on the remaining months. Read the foreclosure terms if you think you might close early.

### Returns and cancellations

If you return a product bought on EMI, the refund goes to your card, but the EMI conversion may not reverse cleanly. You may need to contact your bank to close the EMI, and the processing fee is often not refunded. Keep the order and EMI reference numbers until the return is complete.

## When No Cost EMI makes sense, and when it does not

**It can make sense when:**

- You would otherwise drain your savings or emergency fund to pay in full.
- The full-payment price and the EMI total are within a few hundred rupees of each other.
- The tenure is short and you are confident of paying every instalment on time.
- The bank waives the processing fee for the sale.

**It is usually not worth it when:**

- You lose a bigger bank discount by choosing EMI over full payment.
- The EMI would push your card close to its limit for months.
- You are buying several things on EMI at once, and the combined monthly amount strains your budget.
- You are using EMI to buy something you would not buy if you had to pay in full.

## Quick checklist on the payment page

1. Note the final price for full payment, after the bank discount.
2. Select No Cost EMI and note the monthly amount and tenure.
3. Look for the processing fee and the interest line in the EMI breakdown.
4. Add GST on interest (18% of the interest shown) and the fee to the EMI total.
5. Compare the two totals. Choose the lower one if you can afford it.
6. Check your available credit limit after the EMI amount is blocked.

If the payment page does not show the interest or fee clearly, check your bank's EMI terms or the SMS and statement your bank sends after conversion, which list these charges.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is No Cost EMI really free?
No Cost EMI is not completely free. The seller covers the interest through an upfront discount, but you usually pay 18% GST on that interest and sometimes a processing fee. On a ₹60,000 purchase over six months, this can add up to around ₹700, depending on your bank.

### Why do I pay GST on No Cost EMI?
You pay GST because the bank still charges interest on the EMI loan, and GST at 18% applies to that interest. The seller's discount covers the interest itself, but not the GST on it.

### Does the bank instant discount apply on No Cost EMI?
It depends on the offer. For Amazon Great Indian Festival 2026, Amazon has said the 10% SBI card discount applies to EMI transactions as well. Other offers may have a different cap or minimum order for EMI, so compare the full-payment and EMI totals on the payment page.

### Does No Cost EMI affect my credit score?
No Cost EMI can affect your credit score. The full purchase amount is blocked on your credit card, which raises your credit utilisation. Paying every instalment on time helps your score, and missing one can lower it.

### Can I cancel or prepay a No Cost EMI?
You can usually prepay or cancel, but there may be a foreclosure fee and you may lose the No Cost benefit on the remaining instalments. If you return the product, contact your bank to close the EMI, because the processing fee is often not refunded.
